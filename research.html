Research

Mathematical modeling and applied analysis of PDE models for complex biological and porous media.

Overview

My research lies at the interface of applied analysis and mathematical modeling. I study partial differential equation models of complex biological and porous media, with two main goals: establishing their well-posedness, and rigorously deriving effective macroscopic descriptions through homogenization and asymptotic limits.

A particular focus is on biphasic mixture and visco-morphoelastic models, which describe the mechanics of growing and remodelling biological tissues and tissue-engineering constructs.

Homogenization & multiscale analysis

Biological tissues and porous materials have a fine microstructure. Homogenization theory provides rigorous tools for passing from microscopic models to effective macroscopic equations as the scale parameter $\varepsilon \to 0$, for instance through two-scale expansions

$$u_\varepsilon(x) \approx u_0\!\left(x,\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon}\right) + \varepsilon\, u_1\!\left(x,\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon}\right) + \cdots$$

Biphasic mixture models

Soft tissues and scaffolds are often modelled as mixtures of a deformable solid matrix and an interstitial fluid. I study the well-posedness and homogenization of such biphasic mixture models arising in biological and tissue-engineering systems.

Visco-morphoelastic systems

Morphoelasticity describes materials that grow and remodel. My current work analyses an incompressible visco-morphoelastic system: its weak solutions and its vanishing-diffusion limit, in which objective stress rates such as the Jaumann derivative play a central role,

$$\overset{\circ}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} = \dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} + \boldsymbol{\sigma}\mathbf{W} - \mathbf{W}\boldsymbol{\sigma}.$$

Analysis of nonlinear PDEs

Existence of weak solutions, compactness arguments and singular limits for nonlinear PDE systems, together with variational methods for non-local problems that lack compactness.
